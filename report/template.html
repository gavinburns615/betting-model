Refresh data

This page is a saved snapshot — a web page like this can't pull live odds from inside your browser, so the refresh happens on the computer that built it. Two ways:

  1. Run venv/bin/python3 src/refresh.py in the repo. It re-pulls schedules, final scores and current lines, re-grades any games that have been played, flags lines that moved, and rebuilds this page.
  2. Or ask Claude to "refresh the report" — it runs the same command and republishes the live link.

What a refresh changes: prices, the worth-it check, line-moved flags, and graded results. What it doesn't: which side is picked. That comes from the signal alone, not the spread.

If you only bet the top 2 every week

1 unit, top 2 signal-gap picks/week, flat stake — 2020–2025

Restricting to each week's two largest signal gaps performed much better than betting everything: 62.9% win rate vs. 52.0%, and every one of the six backtested seasons finished positive. It also held up on a genuine holdout — picking this cutoff using only 2020–2022, then testing it on 2023–2025 alone, still gave 62.7% (p = 0.013).

Still worth some skepticism: this comes from one early/late split, not the same full expanding-window walk-forward behind the main result, and the sample is small (202 bets over 6 seasons). Read it as a genuinely promising lead on top of an already-inconclusive signal, not a second, separately proven strategy.

02 — IS IT WORTH BETTING?

The odds are never exactly even — so "worth it" changes bet to bet

A pick being correct and a pick being worth taking are different questions. This section is the math behind the small edge line on every row in §01.

Break-even, not 50%

Sportsbooks price both sides of a bet so that winning less than half the time still loses you money — that's the vig. The win rate you need just to break even (the implied probability) depends on the actual odds, not on 50%:

Our best estimate

The corrected strategy's out-of-sample win rate across 2020–2025 is 52.0%. Using every season of history at once (the number this page's live picks are built on) it's 52.8%.

Neither clears conventional statistical significance — p = 0.14 on the backtested number. Treat this as a weak, unproven lean, not a proven true win rate.

What "worth it" means here

A row is tagged worth it when our 52–53% estimate clears that specific bet's break-even price. It is not worth it when the price is steep enough (favorites priced at −130 or worse, for instance) that even our optimistic estimate doesn't clear the vig.

Historically, — of bets cleared their own break-even under this estimate. That number is inflated by using an estimate that itself might not be real — it is not a claim that betting all of them would have profited.

Bottom line: most individual prices look "worth it" against a 52–53% estimate, because that estimate is only a couple points above the −110 break-even of 52.4%. But the estimate itself carries p = 0.14 — there's a real chance the true edge is zero, in which case none of these are actually worth it, regardless of what the tag says. Use the edge numbers to sort bets by how much room they have, not as a green light.

03 — DATA & SIGNAL

Where every number comes from

01

Games, closing lines & weather

nflverse public schedules (via nfl_data_py), free, no key. Spread, total, moneyline, and odds for both sides — zero missing values across regular-season games, 2016–2025, plus the 2026 schedule as lines get posted week by week.

no opening line available → CLV not computable from this source
02

Presnap predictability signal

Derived from the existing pass/run classifier's own season-level walk-forward predictions (276K plays, 2018–2025) — each season scored by a model trained only on strictly earlier seasons. Summarized per team-game as mean model confidence, then recency-weighted (EWMA span-8) over that team's prior games only.

verified by direct recomputation: entering-value is a pure function of weeks < W
03

Face-validity check

2023 Ravens/Eagles (Jackson, Hurts — RPO-heavy, dual-threat) score as the least predictable offenses in the whole dataset despite skewed run rates, because the signal reads situational disguise, not raw run/pass mix. That's a different, more specific claim than a naive pass-rate stat would make — and it's the one the strategy is actually staking on.

04

Strategy rule, direction locked from history

signal_diff = home confidence − away confidence. Bet the side signal_diff favors — home when it's positive, away when negative — with the sign itself fixed by fitting on all backtested seasons (2018–2025) before ever looking at a future game.

05

Live vs. backtested convention

The historical picks below reset each team's signal to zero information at the start of every season (so week 1 is never bet, matching how the classifier itself was validated). Weekly picks for 2026 instead carry each team's signal forward continuously from its last played game — otherwise week 1 of a live season would never have a pick at all.
